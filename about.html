About

I make hardware behave, and I can show you the numbers.

I write the software that runs inside industrial and wireless devices: the drivers, the real-time scheduling, the radio links and the recovery paths that keep a product working after it has left the building. I have taken devices from a bare board to production more than once, and spent the hard weeks in between on the field failures nobody could reproduce.

What I care about is evidence. A system is fast when the trace says so, reliable when the logs from a year in the field say so, and finished when someone else can build it from the repository. The projects on this site are built that way: every claim links to a measurement.

Recent work

Two projects, with the receipts

lumo-watch

A dual-core Zephyr smartwatch on a round AMOLED whose second hand glides at a locked 60 fps, and which doubles as a USB volume knob for the laptop.

  • 12 fps with a GUI library to 59, steady, with a hand-written compositor
  • Display writes 3.4 ms to 0.5 ms by driving the SPI and DMA from the registers
  • Every optimisation has a build switch and a before/after trace from the device

lumo-notes

A pocket voice recorder with a puppy in it: notes and reminders by voice, transcribed by Whisper on a laptop in the same room, with a meeting mode that transcribes live.

  • Fifteen threads on one core, one owner per device, message passing only
  • Zero-copy audio: one buffer, three owners, two slots
  • A kernel-level tracer that animates buffers filling and threads taking turns

How I work

A few rules I keep coming back to

Measure before you guess.

Cycle counters, traces and captured logs first. The slow part is rarely where it feels like it is; the watch's display cost went from 315 to 107 cycles per pixel only after the numbers said where to look.

One owner per device.

A peripheral belongs to exactly one thread; everyone else sends it a message. It removes a whole class of bugs and makes the system drawable on one page.

Design for the worst frame.

Averages lie. A steady 30 looks better than an average 59 that stutters, so budgets are set on the worst case and the trace has to show it.

Write it down, with the data.

Dead ends included. The write-ups here keep the raw captures next to the conclusions, so the next person, often me, can check the reasoning.
